# 1 · MEMBER SPONSORSHIP & GUEST ACCOUNTABILITY

**The single most effective behavior mechanism a private club has is not a rule. It is a member who has to see everyone again next month.**

## 1.1 The principle

Guests attend as the guest of a specific member. **That member is accountable for their guest's conduct as if it were their own.** This is not a formality — sponsorship consequences are real and are enforced.

## 1.2 Guest allowances

| Tier | Guests per sailing | Guests per season |
|---|---|---|
| `[Tier 1]` | `[N]` | `[N]` |
| `[Tier 2]` | `[N]` | `[N]` |
| `[Tier 3]` | `[N]` | `[N]` |

Allowances do not carry over. Unused guest slots expire with the season.

## 1.3 Sponsoring a guest

The sponsoring member must:
- Book the guest under their own account, by name, in advance
- Confirm the guest meets age and eligibility requirements
- **Send the guest the Code of Conduct, Prohibited Items, and What to Expect before the sailing** — a guest who breaks a rule they never saw is a sponsorship failure, not a guest failure
- Sail on the same sailing. **A member may not sponsor a guest onto a sailing they are not attending.**
- Remain with and responsible for the guest throughout

## 1.4 What the sponsor is accountable for

Conduct · compliance with all policies · damage caused · fees, charges, and gratuities · the guest's fitness to board · any consequences arising from the guest's presence.

## 1.5 Sponsor consequences

| Guest outcome | Sponsor consequence |
|---|---|
| Level 1–3 discipline | Noted; no action |
| Level 4 — removal from sailing | Documented warning to sponsor |
| Level 5 — guest suspended | Sponsor's guest privileges suspended `[90 days]` |
| Level 6 — guest permanently revoked | Sponsor's guest privileges revoked `[12 months]`; membership review |
| Zero Tolerance violation | Sponsor's guest privileges revoked `[12 months]` minimum; membership review; sponsor may face revocation where they facilitated or concealed the conduct |

**Two Level 4+ guest incidents within `[12 months]` suspend a member's guest privileges entirely for the remainder of the season.**

## 1.6 What protects the sponsor

A sponsor who identifies a problem early and brings it to the crew is treated as having discharged their responsibility, not as having caused it. **Sponsors who flag a guest before something happens face no consequence.** Sponsors who cover for a guest, dispute a crew decision, or misrepresent what happened face the same consequence as the guest.

## 1.7 Guests may not sponsor

Only members in good standing sponsor. A guest cannot bring a guest. Chains of unaccountable attendance are how a members' club becomes a public party.

## 1.8 Repeat guests

A guest attending more than `[3]` times in a season is invited to apply for membership. `[Set an escalating guest fee or a conversion requirement — this is where a club leaks value.]`

## 1.9 Standing invitations

A member may not offer open invitations, post guest availability publicly, or sell, trade, or transfer guest slots for consideration. Guest slots are a membership benefit, not a currency.

# 3 · TALENT, VENDOR & CONTRACTOR CONDUCT RIDER

**Attach to every agreement with a DJ, performer, photographer, caterer, brand partner, or contractor working aboard.**

---

## 3.1 You are bound by the guest policies too

Every person working aboard M/V Sea Charm II — talent, crew for hire, media, vendors, and their assistants — is bound by the **Code of Conduct, Prohibited Items, Zero Tolerance Policies, and Emergency Procedures** in full. There is no professional exemption. In practice the standard applied to working personnel is *higher* than the one applied to guests, because you are here in a professional capacity and guests take their cues from you.

## 3.2 The Master's authority extends to you

The Master has full authority over every person aboard, including you and your team, without exception. Instructions are followed immediately. The Master may curtail a set, cut sound, restrict movement, or remove any person for any safety reason.

**This includes stopping your performance.** Sound goes to zero for safety announcements, docking, emergencies, and noise compliance, without warning and without negotiation. Build this into your expectations and your contract.

## 3.3 You count against capacity

Every member of your team occupies a berth against the vessel's **COI limit of `[COI CAPACITY]`**. Team size must be confirmed in writing at least `[7]` days in advance and **cannot be increased on the day.** An unannounced assistant at the gangway does not board. Every team member appears on the manifest with full name, date of birth, and emergency contact.

## 3.4 Load-in and equipment

- Equipment list submitted `[7]` days in advance and approved by the Master
- Load-in window `[TIME]`; load-out `[TIME]` — the dock is shared and slots are tight
- **All equipment must be secured against vessel motion.** Nothing freestanding, nothing unsecured, nothing that becomes a projectile in a wake.
- Power draw confirmed in advance. `[Generators are prohibited.]`
- Nothing may obstruct aisles, exits, gangways, rails, life jacket stowage, fire equipment, or the helm
- **Weight and placement must be approved by the Master** — distribution is a live stability factor on a pontoon platform
- Marine-appropriate gear only. Salt, spray, and humidity are your problem, and the Company is not liable for damage to your equipment.

## 3.5 Prohibited for talent and vendors

Everything on the guest Prohibited Items list, plus: **pyrotechnics, hazers, foggers, and CO2 cannons** `[unless expressly approved in writing with fire safety review]` · confetti and glitter of any kind, including biodegradable · lasers · drones · open flame · glass of any kind, including glass-bodied equipment · generators · unapproved rigging or overhead suspension.

## 3.6 Alcohol and substances

**Talent and vendors do not consume alcohol while working.** `[Set the standard for after a set concludes — permitted at guest level, or not at all.]` Zero tolerance for controlled substances applies identically and without any allowance for industry norms.

## 3.7 Conduct toward guests and crew

Professional at all times. No harassment, no pursuit of guests, no soliciting personal contact information. **You may not pursue romantic or sexual contact with guests during an engagement.** No disparaging the Company, crew, venue, or guests, aboard or online.

## 3.8 Content and media

- You may capture content of your own performance `[subject to approval]`
- **You must respect guest media opt-outs.** Guests wearing `[OPT-OUT MARKER]` may not appear in your content.
- Do not photograph or film guests without their agreement
- Content featuring the vessel, brand, or marks requires `[written approval / approval before posting]`
- Grant of license to the Company for content produced under the engagement: `[SET TERMS]`
- No posting during an incident, emergency, or casualty, ever

## 3.10 Independent contractor status

Contractors are not employees, agents, or partners of the Company, and may not represent otherwise. Responsible for their own taxes, insurance, and team.

## 3.11 Safety obligations

Attend the safety orientation in full · know the exits, life jacket stowage, and muster station · brief your own team on emergency signals · **stop work immediately on any emergency signal** · report every injury and near-miss before leaving the dock.

## 3.12 Weather

Sailings cancel for weather. `[State the cancellation and payment terms — deposit retained, full fee, rescheduling right. Talent agreements that ignore weather cancellation in South Florida generate a dispute roughly once a season.]`

## 3.13 Confidentiality

Member identities, membership lists, guest information, and business terms are confidential. **Do not disclose who was aboard.** For a members' club, discretion is a substantial part of what members are actually paying for, and a single indiscreet post can cost more than an engagement is worth.

## 3.14 Breach

Immediate termination of the engagement, removal at the first safe opportunity, forfeiture of unpaid fees, removal from the approved vendor list, and liability for damage caused.
