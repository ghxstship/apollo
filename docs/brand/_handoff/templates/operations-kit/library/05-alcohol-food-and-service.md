# 1 · ALCOHOL SERVICE POLICY

> **RESOLVE THE LICENSING MODEL BEFORE PUBLISHING ANY VERSION OF THIS DOCUMENT.**
>
> Florida's special excursion, sightseeing, and charter boat liquor permit under **Fla. Stat. § 565.02(3)(a)** requires a **USCG-approved capacity of at least 125 passengers.** The Trident 4512 cannot reach that threshold under any configuration. That route is closed to you.
>
> The alternative in the same statute: operators of pleasure, excursion, sightseeing, and charter boats may obtain a **standard vendor license with the vessel designated as the place of business**, subject to all requirements applicable to consumption-on-premises vendors. That means a quota or non-quota consumption license (4COP/COP variants), full DBPR compliance, and a licensed premises that happens to float.
>
> **The trap:** if alcohol is "included" in membership dues or ticket price rather than sold by the drink, DBPR may still treat the transaction as a sale. "Free with admission" is a well-worn theory that has not historically fared well. Do not build the business model on it without a written opinion.
>
> **Choose one of the three models below with counsel, delete the other two, and set every `[ALCOHOL MODEL]` marker across the library to match.**

## MODEL A — LICENSED SERVICE

*The Company holds a Florida beverage license, the vessel is the licensed premises, and alcohol is sold by the drink aboard.*

### A.1 Who may be served
Guests **21 or older** with valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID, verified at check-in and available for re-verification at any point.

### A.2 Refusal is mandatory, not discretionary
Crew **must** refuse or discontinue service to any guest who:
- Cannot produce acceptable ID
- Appears intoxicated by any observable indicator
- Is suspected of passing drinks to a minor or to a cut-off guest
- Has been cut off earlier in the sailing

Refusal is a legal obligation under Florida law, not a hospitality judgment. **Management supports every refusal without exception, and no refusal is reversed in front of a guest.**

### A.3 Service limits
- Maximum `[1]` drink served per guest per transaction
- No doubles, no shots `[SET POLICY]`, no bottle service, no self-service
- Minimum `[15]` minutes between transactions to the same guest `[IF USING PACING]`
- Service ends `[30]` minutes before scheduled return to dock
- Free water available at all times without asking, and food available whenever alcohol is `[IF REQUIRED BY LICENSE CLASS]`

### A.4 Dram shop exposure
Under **Fla. Stat. § 768.125**, a vendor is generally shielded from liability for injury caused by an intoxicated patron — **except** where the vendor willfully served a minor, or knowingly served a person habitually addicted to alcohol. Those two exceptions are the entire risk surface, and both are behavioral. Train to them.

### A.5 Responsible vendor status
The Company `[participates / intends to participate]` in the Florida Responsible Vendor Program (**Fla. Stat. § 561.705**). All service staff complete an approved responsible vendor training course before their first sailing and refresher training every `[12 months]`. Training records are retained.

### A.6 Documentation
Every refusal and cut-off is logged: sailing, guest, time, indicator observed, crew member, witness. This log is your defense if a claim ever arises. Keep it contemporaneously, not afterward.

## MODEL B — BYOB

*Guests bring their own alcohol. The Company sells none, provides none, and includes none in any fee.*

### B.1 The line the Company must not cross
No sale. No inclusion in dues or ticket price. No "donation" in exchange for drinks. No stocking, pouring, mixing, or serving by crew. **Crew do not handle guest alcohol at any point.** The moment the Company touches the supply chain, the licensing analysis changes. `ATTORNEY — confirm the BYOB posture holds under Florida law given a paid membership model.`

### B.2 Container rules
- **Aluminum cans, plastic bottles, and pouches only. No glass, ever.** See Prohibited Items.
- One soft-sided cooler bag per guest, `[12-quart]` or smaller
- Personal quantity limit: `[6]` standard drinks per guest
- No kegs, party balls, or communal dispensers
- Contents subject to inspection at the gangway

### B.3 Guest responsibilities
- 21+ only, ID verified at check-in
- Consume your own; **do not share with anyone whose age you have not personally verified**
- Manage your own pace — there is no bartender monitoring you, which makes this entirely your responsibility
- Pack out every container. Nothing overboard.

### B.4 The Company's retained authority
The Master and crew may at any time direct a guest to stop consuming, confiscate and hold alcohol for the remainder of the sailing, or remove a guest for impairment. **BYOB is not a waiver of the Company's authority over conduct aboard.**

### B.5 The honest warning to guests
Self-managed drinking on a boat is harder than it looks. Sun, heat, motion, and dehydration compound alcohol substantially faster than the same drinks ashore. A guest who is fine at the dock can be a genuine safety problem an hour offshore. Pace yourself and drink water between every one.

## MODEL C — DRY

*No alcohol aboard, from any source.*

### C.1 The rule
No alcohol is sold, served, provided, or permitted aboard M/V Sea Charm II. Guests may not bring alcohol. Alcohol found at screening is refused. Guests who arrive impaired are denied boarding without refund.

### C.2 Why an operator would choose this
It eliminates licensing exposure, dram shop exposure, the largest single driver of conduct incidents on passenger vessels, and most of the insurance conversation. If the club's draw is the water, the design, and the room rather than the bar, dry is a genuinely strong position — and it opens family and daytime programming without a second policy set.

## Applies to every model

### 1.6 The Master and crew
**Operating crew do not consume alcohol before or during any sailing.** Credentialed crew are subject to mandatory drug and alcohol testing under 46 CFR Part 16 — see the Crew Drug & Alcohol Testing Policy. This is not a house rule; it is a condition of the vessel's certificate.

### 1.7 Minors
Furnishing alcohol to anyone under 21 is a **felony under Fla. Stat. § 562.11** and a Zero Tolerance violation resulting in immediate removal and law enforcement referral, for both the supplier and the recipient.

### 1.8 Impairment
Guests who cannot stand unassisted, respond coherently, or manage themselves aboard are seated, monitored, and removed at the first safe opportunity. This is a Zero Tolerance determination and the Master's call is final.

### 1.9 Getting home
The Company `[provides / arranges / does not arrange]` shoreside transport. **Guests are responsible for arranging a sober ride.** Boating under the influence and driving under the influence are both prosecuted vigorously in Miami-Dade and Broward. `[RECOMMENDED: publish rideshare pickup instructions for the marina in the What to Expect guide.]`

## 2.3 Temperature and time control

The single hardest problem in marine food service is heat. On an open deck in a Miami summer, the safe holding window collapses.

- Cold foods held at or below **41°F** · hot foods at or above **135°F**
- Insulated transport from commissary to vessel, temperature logged at loading and at service
- **Any food held in the temperature danger zone (41–135°F) for more than 2 hours is discarded**, and in direct sun the working standard is `[1 hour]`
- Discarded food is logged, not quietly re-served
- No re-service of food returned from a guest
- Shaded or covered service station required

## 2.4 Allergens

**We cannot guarantee an allergen-free environment.** The galley and service area are small, shared, and moving. Cross-contact is a real risk that no amount of care fully eliminates aboard a vessel this size.

- The **nine major allergens** — milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame — are identified on every menu
- Guests with allergies must disclose at booking, not at the buffet
- Advance notice of `[72]` hours allows us to arrange a separately prepared meal where possible
- **Guests with severe or anaphylactic allergies should carry their own epinephrine auto-injector and inform the crew at check-in.** Emergency medical response offshore is delayed by definition — this matters more here than in a restaurant.

## 2.5 Dietary accommodation

Vegetarian, vegan, halal, kosher `[state what is genuinely achievable]`, gluten-free `[note whether cross-contact-safe or merely gluten-free ingredients — the distinction matters to celiac guests and misrepresenting it is dangerous]`. Request at booking with `[72]` hours notice.

## 2.6 Guest-supplied food

`[PERMITTED / NOT PERMITTED]`. Where permitted: sealed, personal-portion, non-glass containers only, for the bringer's own consumption. **Guests may not share homemade food with other guests** — undisclosed allergens in shared food are a genuine hazard and the Company cannot supervise it.

## 2.7 Water and ice

## 2.8 Waste

All food waste is retained aboard and offloaded dockside. **Nothing goes over the side — not scraps, not ice, not fruit.** Federal law prohibits it, and it draws wildlife into vessel traffic.

## 2.9 Illness reporting

Any suspected foodborne illness is reported to `[SAFETY OFFICER]` immediately, logged, and reported to the Florida Department of Health where required. Suspect product is held, not discarded, pending investigation.

# 3 · GRATUITY POLICY

## 3.1 The stance

`[CHOOSE ONE]`

**Option 1 — Gratuity included.** A `[20%]` service charge is included in the fare and distributed to crew and service staff. No additional tipping is expected. Additional gratuity is welcome but never solicited.

**Option 2 — Discretionary.** Gratuity is not included. If the crew took care of you, `[15–20%]` is customary in the Miami charter trade. `[Cash / card / QR]` accepted at `[LOCATION]`.

**Option 3 — No tipping.** Crew are compensated at `[a rate that removes the need for gratuity]`. We don't accept tips, and guests should not feel any obligation.

> **Recommendation:** Option 1 or 3. Discretionary tipping in a members' club creates a service dynamic where crew have a financial incentive that competes with their safety authority — the deckhand who cuts off a heavy tipper should not be doing arithmetic while making that call. Removing the tension is worth the pricing complexity.

## 3.2 Distribution

Gratuities and service charges are distributed among crew and service staff per `[DISTRIBUTION SCHEDULE]`. **The Master and licensed operating crew `[participate / do not participate]`** in gratuity distribution. `[Consider excluding the Master. The person who decides whether to cancel a swim call or cut someone off should have no financial stake in guest satisfaction that day.]`

## 3.3 What we never do

- Solicit, pressure, or shame guests regarding gratuity
- Announce tip amounts or identify tippers
- Vary service quality by tip expectation
- Permit crew to accept gratuity in exchange for bending any policy — that is a disciplinary matter, not a gray area

## 3.4 Transparency

All service charges are disclosed at booking, itemized on the receipt, and never presented as a tax or a mandatory fee if they are neither.

## 3.5 Contractors and talent

DJs, performers, photographers, and vendors are compensated under their own agreements. Tipping them is entirely at guest discretion. See the Talent, Vendor & Contractor Conduct Rider.
