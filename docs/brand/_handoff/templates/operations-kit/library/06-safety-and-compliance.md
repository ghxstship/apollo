# 1 · INCIDENT REPORTING & MARINE CASUALTY PROCEDURE

## 1.1 Report everything

**Every injury, near-miss, equipment failure, conduct incident, and unusual event is reported before the crew goes home.** Not the serious ones — all of them. An undocumented minor injury becomes a disputed major claim eighteen months later, and near-misses are the only free information you will ever get about the accident you haven't had yet.

## 1.2 Immediate response

| Priority | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Ensure life safety. Treat injuries. Prevent escalation. |
| 2 | Master assumes control of the scene and directs response. |
| 3 | Notify the Coast Guard on **VHF 16** if there is danger to life, the vessel, or the environment. |
| 4 | Account for all persons aboard. Full head count. |
| 5 | Preserve the scene and evidence. Photograph. Do not clean up or repair beyond what safety requires. |
| 6 | Secure witness names and contact details **before anyone disembarks.** After they scatter, they are gone. |
| 7 | Notify `[SAFETY OFFICER]` and `[CLUB PHONE]`. |

## 1.3 Coast Guard casualty reporting

Under **46 CFR Part 4**, the Company must notify the nearest Coast Guard Sector **immediately** and file **Form CG-2692 within five days** of any of the following:

- Death
- Injury requiring professional medical treatment beyond first aid, or that renders a person unfit to perform routine duties
- Loss of life or a person missing from the vessel
- Grounding
- Loss of main propulsion, primary steering, or any associated component that reduces the vessel's maneuverability
- Occurrence materially and adversely affecting the vessel's seaworthiness or fitness for service
- Loss of the vessel
- Property damage in excess of the current regulatory threshold `[VERIFY CURRENT DOLLAR FIGURE — it has been amended]`
- Discharge of oil or a hazardous substance

**When in doubt, report.** Under-reporting a casualty is a far worse position than over-reporting one.

## 1.4 Drug and alcohol testing after a serious marine incident

A **serious marine incident** triggers mandatory chemical testing of all directly involved crew under **46 CFR Part 4** and Part 16:

- **Alcohol testing within 2 hours** of the incident (extendable to 8 with documented reason)
- **Drug testing within 32 hours**
- Applies to any crew member whose acts or failures may have contributed

Post-incident testing capability must be arranged **before the first sailing**, not looked up during the emergency. Identify your collection site and after-hours provider now: `[COLLECTION SITE / 24-HOUR PROVIDER]`

## 1.5 Internal report contents

Filed within **24 hours** by the Master or senior crew present:

Date · time · position · weather and sea state · vessel condition · persons involved with contact details · witnesses with contact details · sequence of events in plain chronological narrative · injuries and treatment given · equipment involved · photographs · actions taken · notifications made and to whom · immediate corrective action.

**Write what happened.** Do not write conclusions about fault, do not speculate on cause, and do not editorialize. The report is a factual record that will be read by insurers, investigators, and possibly a court.

## 1.6 Escalation

| Severity | Notify | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** — death, missing person, serious injury, fire, flooding, grounding, collision | USCG · 911 · `[SAFETY OFFICER]` · `[PRINCIPAL]` · insurer | Immediately |
| **Major** — injury requiring transport, MOB recovered, propulsion loss, significant conduct incident | `[SAFETY OFFICER]` · insurer | Within 2 hours |
| **Moderate** — first aid injury, equipment failure, service refusal, removal | `[SAFETY OFFICER]` | Same day |
| **Minor** — near-miss, minor equipment issue, guest complaint | `[SAFETY OFFICER]` | Within 24 hours via log |

## 1.7 Insurance

Notify the carrier per policy terms — usually immediately for any potential claim. `[CARRIER · POLICY NUMBER · 24-HOUR CLAIMS LINE]`

## 1.8 What crew must not do

- Admit fault, apologize in terms implying liability, or speculate about cause to guests, media, or anyone other than the Master and `[SAFETY OFFICER]`
- Discuss the incident on social media, in any form, ever
- Speak to media — refer all inquiries to `[MEDIA CONTACT]`
- Alter, clean, or repair the scene beyond what is required for safety
- Delete photographs, messages, or logs

## 1.9 No-blame near-miss reporting

Near-misses may be reported without disciplinary consequence, including by the crew member who made the error. **The only near-miss that costs someone their job is the one they concealed.** This is the single most valuable safety mechanism a small operation has, and it only works if leadership means it every time.

## 1.10 Review and corrective action

`[SAFETY OFFICER]` reviews every incident within `[7]` days, identifies contributing factors, assigns corrective actions with owners and dates, and reports trends to `[PRINCIPAL]` `[quarterly]`. Corrective actions are tracked to closure.

## 1.11 Records

Retained **`[7]` years** minimum, longer where litigation is reasonably anticipated. `Confirm retention against carrier requirements and applicable limitation periods.`

# 2 · CREW DRUG & ALCOHOL TESTING POLICY

**Required under 46 CFR Part 16 for crew serving on inspected vessels. Not discretionary.**

## 2.1 Who is covered

All crew members holding a Coast Guard credential or performing safety-sensitive functions aboard M/V Sea Charm II, including the Master, mates, deckhands, and engineers. `[Determine whether non-credentialed service staff are covered — the regulation reaches persons on board "who perform safety-sensitive functions," which may include more of the crew than expected.]`

## 2.2 Required testing categories

| Category | Trigger |
|---|---|
| **Pre-employment** | Before first performing a safety-sensitive function. Negative result required. |
| **Random** | Unannounced, throughout the year, at the current federally mandated minimum annual rate `[VERIFY CURRENT RATE — it is set annually by USCG]`. Selection must be genuinely random. |
| **Serious marine incident** | Mandatory for all directly involved crew. Alcohol within 2 hours, drugs within 32 hours. |

The federal five-panel: marijuana, cocaine, opiates, phencyclidine, and amphetamines. `[VERIFY current panel composition — DOT panels have been amended.]`

**Marijuana is a positive result regardless of state law or a Florida medical marijuana card.** There is no medical review exception for cannabis under the federal program. Crew must understand this before hire, in writing.

# 3 · PASSENGER SAFETY ORIENTATION SCRIPT

**Required under 46 CFR § 185.506 before getting underway, or as soon as practicable thereafter.**

> Script it. Deliver it identically every sailing. The purpose is not to satisfy an inspector — it is that a person who has heard clear instructions once will act on them under stress, and a person who half-listened to an ad-libbed mumble will not.

---

## 3.2 Regulatory content requirements

Under § 185.506, the announcement must cover:

1. Location of emergency exits, survival craft embarkation areas, and ring life buoys
2. Stowage locations of life jackets, including child life jackets
3. Proper method of donning and adjusting life jackets of the type carried, **including a demonstration** (or notice that a crew member will demonstrate on request, for oceans or coastwise voyages)
4. Location of life jacket and lifesaving device instruction placards
5. That all passengers will be required to don life jackets when hazardous conditions exist, as directed by the Master
6. Where further emergency information is located — the card, pamphlet, or placard at each seat

**Additionally:** passengers boarding after the initial announcement must receive the same information (§ 185.506(d)).

---

## 3.3 Delivery standard

- Delivered by `[the Master or designated crew member]` over the PA, from a position where the speaker is visible
- **Music off. Service paused. Everyone seated.**
- With a physical life jacket in hand for the demonstration — not described, demonstrated
- Duration: approximately 4 minutes
- Repeated in `[Spanish]` where a qualified crew member is aboard
- Late boarders receive an individual briefing from a crew member before the vessel gets underway
- **Logged every sailing:** time delivered, by whom, language, and confirmation that all passengers were present

---

## 3.4 Script

> Good `[afternoon]`, everyone, and welcome aboard Sea Charm II. I'm `[NAME]`, and I'm the `[Captain / mate]` today. Before we get underway I need about four minutes of your full attention. This is required by the Coast Guard, but more to the point, it's the information you'll want to already have if anything goes wrong. **Please pause your conversations and look up.**
>
> **Your crew.** `[Names]` are your crew today. We're wearing `[IDENTIFIER]`. If you need anything, or you see anything that concerns you, come to us straight away.
>
> **Exits.** There are `[N]` exits from this deck: `[point to each]`. Please look at them now.
>
> **Life jackets.** Adult life jackets are stowed `[point]`. Child life jackets are stowed separately `[point]`. Ring life buoys are located `[point]`. **Look at those locations — don't just listen to me say them.**
>
> **This is how you put one on.** `[Physically demonstrate: over the head, straps secured, buckles closed, pulled snug.]` Over your head. Buckle here. Pull the straps snug — snugger than feels comfortable, because a loose jacket rides up over your face in the water. Instruction placards are posted `[point]`, and there's a safety card `[at your seat / in the pocket]`.
>
> **If conditions get rough, I'll ask everyone to put a life jacket on.** When I do that, do it immediately and without asking why. It will not be a drill and it will not be a suggestion.
>
> **Emergency signals.** Seven or more short blasts followed by one long blast means general emergency — stop, go to `[muster location]`, wait for instructions. Three long blasts means someone is in the water — point at them, keep pointing, and don't look away. Continuous sounding means fire or immediate danger — move away from it toward `[muster location]`.
>
> **If someone goes in the water:** shout it out loud, point at them, and **keep pointing.** Do not go in after them. We are trained and equipped for that; you are not, and a second person in the water makes everything worse.
>
> **Moving around.** Stay seated while we're underway. When you do move, use the handrails — one hand for you, one hand for the boat. **Do not sit on the rails, the bow, the transom, or the swim platform while we're moving.** That's the one that hurts people.
>
> **Swimming.** If we have a swim call today, it happens only when I announce it, the engines are off, and the ladder is down. Not before. When I call you back in, come in right away — I'll be counting heads.
>
> **The head is `[location]`.** Please don't put anything in it that you didn't eat first.
>
> **Two more things.** Nothing goes over the side — no trash, no cans, no cigarettes. And drink water. The sun out here is stronger than it feels, and dehydration sneaks up on people fast.
>
> **If anyone boarded late or didn't catch any of that, find me before we leave the dock.** Otherwise — we're going to have a great day. Thanks for listening.

**Assign passenger control explicitly.** On a small passenger vessel, the crew member whose job is keeping forty people calm and seated is doing work at least as critical as the one operating equipment — and it is the duty most often left off the bill.

## 4.2 Emergency instructions (§§ 185.510, 185.512)

Emergency instructions must be posted. § 185.512 provides a recommended format covering, at minimum: fire, person overboard, flooding, abandon ship, and severe weather — each with the sequence of actions for crew, including maneuvering to minimize wind effect on a fire, launching a rescue boat or maneuvering to recover a person in the water, and notifying the Coast Guard and vessels in the vicinity by radiotelephone if a person is not immediately located.
