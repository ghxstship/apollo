# DOCUMENT 1 — ALLOWED ITEMS

**What you may bring aboard M/V Sea Charm II**

We run a small vessel with fixed capacity, limited stowage, and a Coast Guard-inspected deck plan. Everything you bring has to fit under your seat or in your lap, and nothing may block an aisle, exit, rail, or equipment locker. Pack like you're boarding a small aircraft, not loading a car.

## Bring this

**Bags and stowage**
- One soft-sided bag, tote, or backpack per guest, roughly 18" × 14" × 8" or smaller
- One soft-sided cooler bag per guest, roughly 12-quart or smaller, if the event is designated `[BYO / BYOB]`

**Sun and weather**
- Sunscreen in **lotion, stick, or cream form** — reef-conscious formulations appreciated
- Hat, visor, or cap (secure it; we do not turn around for hats)
- Sunglasses, ideally with a retainer strap
- Light cover-up, sarong, or long sleeve layer
- Compact rain shell

**On your body**
- Swimwear worn under or as your outfit
- **Flat, non-marking, non-slip footwear** — boat shoes, deck sneakers, rubber-soled sandals with a heel strap. Bare feet are fine on the deck.
- One towel per guest

**Personal and medical**
- Prescription medication in its original labeled container
- Rescue inhalers, EpiPens, insulin, and other emergency medication — please also tell us at check-in
- Motion sickness remedies (take them *before* boarding, not after)
- Contact lens or glasses backups

**Electronics**
- Phone or camera, ideally tethered or in a floating waterproof pouch
- Handheld action camera
- Power bank
- Hearing aids, medical wearables, personal cooling devices

**Hydration and food**
- Reusable, **non-glass** water bottle — refill stations are available onboard
- Canned or plastic-bottled non-alcoholic beverages
- Sealed personal snacks, unless the event is catered and marked "no outside food"
- Where the event is designated `[BYOB]`: beverages in **aluminum cans, pouches, or plastic only** `[ALCOHOL MODEL — delete if licensed service or dry]`

**Safety**
- Your own USCG-approved Type I, II, III, or V personal flotation device, correctly sized, **in addition to** the vessel's inventory — never as a substitute for it
- Required for guests under 18: a properly fitted, USCG-approved PFD in the child's weight range. If you don't bring one, we will fit one from ship's stock before departure.

**Payment and ID**
- Government-issued photo ID — **required for every guest, every sailing, no exceptions**
- Cards, contactless payment, small cash for gratuities

## Please leave ashore
Anything not listed here, and everything on the Prohibited Items list. If you're unsure, ask before you pack: `[CLUB EMAIL]`.

## Two things worth knowing
- **We are not a checked-bag operation.** There is no secure storage. Leave valuables, heirlooms, and anything you would grieve at home. See our Lost & Found and Personal Property Policy.
- **The Master has final say.** Any item may be refused at the gangway at the Captain's discretion, including items on this list, if it presents a safety, capacity, or weight-distribution concern that day.

# DOCUMENT 2 — PROHIBITED ITEMS

**What may not come aboard M/V Sea Charm II**

Every item below is prohibited for a specific reason — federal law, state law, a Coast Guard requirement, fire risk, or the simple physics of a small vessel offshore. Bags are subject to inspection at the gangway. Bringing a prohibited item is grounds for denied boarding with no refund; concealing one is grounds for permanent membership revocation.

## Absolutely prohibited — no exceptions, ever

**Glass, in any form**
Bottles, stemware, tumblers, jars, glass candle vessels, mirrors, glass-bodied vapes, glass cosmetics. A broken bottle on a wet aluminum deck full of barefoot people is the single most preventable injury in our business. If it is glass, it does not board.

**Controlled substances**
Any illegal drug or controlled substance, in any quantity, plus all associated paraphernalia and nitrous oxide chargers.

**Cannabis and THC products — including Florida medical cannabis**
This vessel operates on the navigable waters of the United States, under federal jurisdiction and Coast Guard authority, where cannabis remains a Schedule I controlled substance. **A Florida medical marijuana card provides no protection aboard this vessel.** This includes flower, edibles, cartridges, tinctures, and topicals. The Coast Guard's zero-tolerance drug policy exposes the vessel itself to seizure over trace quantities — this is not a rule we have any discretion on.

**Weapons**
Firearms of any kind (loaded, unloaded, or disassembled), ammunition, knives with blades over 3 inches or any fixed-blade knife, spearguns, pole spears, machetes, batons, tasers, stun guns, chemical sprays, and replica or novelty weapons. Concealed weapon permits do not apply aboard. Lawfully owned firearms must remain secured in your vehicle ashore.

**Fire, flame, and fuel**
Fireworks, sparklers, sky lanterns, flares (other than the vessel's own inventory), candles, incense, lighters used for anything other than cigarettes in a designated area, portable grills, camp stoves, sterno, butane or propane canisters, lighter fluid, spare fuel, and lithium batteries showing damage or swelling.

**Aerosols and pressurized cans**
Spray sunscreen, hairspray, spray deodorant, dry shampoo, air horns, spray paint, and compressed gas canisters. Aerosols are a fire risk near fuel systems, make the deck dangerously slick, and drift into other guests' faces and drinks. Lotion sunscreen only.

## Prohibited — marine debris and environmental

Federal law (MARPOL Annex V) and Florida law prohibit discharging plastics and refuse into the water. These items inevitably end up overboard:

- Confetti, confetti cannons, glitter, and glitter bombs — including "biodegradable" varieties
- Balloons of any kind, latex or mylar
- Silly string, streamers, sparkle powder, colored powder
- Loose single-use plastics, disposable straws, plastic cutlery
- Cigarette butts discarded overboard (this is littering, and it is enforced)
- Floating body glitter and shimmer oils

## Prohibited — operational and capacity

- **Portable speakers and personal sound systems.** Audio aboard is programmed and controlled from the helm, which must be able to override all sound instantly for safety announcements. Miami-Dade County's noise ordinance also restricts music audible more than 100 feet from the vessel between 11:00 PM and 7:00 AM, and we sail in neighborhoods with residents.
- Drones and remote-controlled aircraft — launching, landing, or operating from the vessel
- Hard-sided coolers, wheeled coolers, kegs, and party balls
- Folding chairs, inflatable furniture, and any non-secured seating
- Large inflatable floats, water toys, and towables not issued by the club
- Professional lighting rigs, generators, extension cords, staging, and outside AV
- Luggage, roller bags, oversized duffels, and hard cases
- Fishing gear, tackle, cast nets, and spearfishing equipment unless the sailing is expressly designated for it
- Anything that would obstruct an aisle, exit, gangway, rail, life jacket locker, fire extinguisher, or the helm

## Prohibited — other

- **Animals**, except trained service animals as defined by the ADA. Emotional support animals, comfort animals, and therapy animals do not qualify. See our Service Animal Policy.
- Body paint, henna, and cosmetics that stain fiberglass, vinyl, or aluminum
- Illegal, counterfeit, or altered identification
- Outside professional camera crews, tripods, gimbals over 24", and commercial recording equipment without prior written media authorization
- Vending, sampling, or promotional merchandise not authorized by [un] Hinged Social
- Sealed containers or packages you are transporting on someone else's behalf

## Screening and refusal

All bags are subject to inspection at the gangway before boarding. We are not required to state a reason for refusing an item. Refused items may not be stored aboard, and [un] Hinged Social will not hold, transport, or return them — plan accordingly. Refusing inspection means refusing boarding.

# DOCUMENT 3 — CODE OF CONDUCT

**M/V Sea Charm II · [un] Hinged Social Event Series**

This is a social club on a working commercial vessel. Those two facts sit together comfortably as long as everyone aboard understands the second one. This Code applies from the moment you arrive at `[HOMEPORT / DOCK]` until you leave the property — including the dock, the parking area, and the walk to your car.

## 1. The Captain's authority is absolute

The Master of the vessel has full legal authority over every person aboard, including crew, members, guests, talent, and vendors. Instructions from the Captain or crew — particularly safety instructions — are followed **immediately and without debate**. You may raise a concern afterward through `[CLUB EMAIL]`. You may not litigate it on deck.

The Captain may refuse boarding, restrict movement, terminate service, or return to dock at any time, for any safety reason, without refund.

## 2. Attend the safety briefing

Every guest attends the pre-departure safety orientation. It runs about four minutes and covers muster stations, life jacket locations, exits, and emergency signals. Guests who are absent, distracted, or unwilling to participate do not sail. This is a federal requirement for inspected passenger vessels, not a formality we're being precious about.

## 3. Move like you're on a boat

- **Stay seated while underway** unless the Captain has announced otherwise.
- Use the handrails. One hand for you, one hand for the boat.
- **Never sit, stand, lean, or ride on rails, gunwales, bow, transom, swim platform, or rooftop deck while the vessel is moving.** This is both a Florida boating violation and the leading cause of catastrophic injury on pontoon vessels.
- No climbing, no jumping from the upper deck, no horseplay near rails.
- Access to the helm, engine spaces, machinery, and crew-only areas is prohibited.
- Wet decks are slippery. Walk.

## 4. Swim calls happen on our terms

Swimming is permitted **only** when the Captain announces an authorized swim call, the engines are shut down, the ladder is deployed, and the swim flag is up. Entering the water outside those conditions endangers you and everyone aboard.

- Head count in, head count out. Answer when your name is called.
- Stay within the marked swim area and in sight of the crew.
- No diving. No jumping from any elevated surface.
- Return immediately on the recall signal.
- Swim call may be cancelled at any time due to current, traffic, weather, water quality, or wildlife.

## 5. Alcohol `[ALCOHOL MODEL]`

- Guests must be **21 or older** to consume alcohol. Valid government photo ID is required at check-in and may be requested again at any point.
- **Providing alcohol to anyone under 21 is a felony in Florida and results in immediate permanent revocation and referral to law enforcement.**
- Crew will refuse or discontinue service to any guest who appears impaired. That decision is not negotiable and is not a judgment about you — it is a legal obligation.
- Guests who arrive visibly intoxicated will be denied boarding without refund.
- No one may bring their own alcohol aboard unless the sailing is expressly designated `[BYOB]`.
- The Captain and operating crew do not consume alcohol before or during any sailing, full stop.

## 6. Treat people well

- **No harassment.** Sexual, verbal, physical, or persistent unwanted attention of any kind. "No," "I'm good," and simply walking away are all complete sentences that require no elaboration.
- **Consent is required** for physical contact, photographs, and video. Ask. Accept the answer.
- **No discrimination** on the basis of race, color, national origin, ethnicity, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age, disability, marital status, pregnancy, or veteran status.
- No slurs, hate speech, threats, intimidation, or aggressive escalation.
- Nudity and sexual activity are prohibited aboard.
- Members are responsible for the conduct of guests they bring.

## 7. Photography and content

[un] Hinged Social may photograph and record aboard for promotional use — this is disclosed at booking and covered by our Photo & Video Release. Guests who prefer not to appear should notify staff at check-in and will be issued a visual opt-out marker.

Guest photography is welcome for personal use. It is not welcome when it is aimed at people who have declined, taken up skirts or into changing areas, or used to record crew, other guests, or incidents for the purpose of harassment. Do not post identifiable images of other guests without their agreement.

## 8. Sound and neighbors

Music is curated and controlled from the helm. Personal speakers are prohibited. We sail past residences, moored vessels, and protected areas, and our operating permission depends on being a tolerable neighbor. Voice levels carry remarkably far over water — further than you think.

## 9. Respect the water

- **Nothing goes overboard.** No trash, no cigarette butts, no food, no ice, no bottles, no gray water. Everything you bring aboard leaves with you or goes in a receptacle.
- The vessel's head discharges to a holding tank and is pumped out dockside. Never attempt to operate any discharge valve.
- Do not feed, chase, touch, or attempt to swim with marine wildlife. Manatees and dolphins are federally protected — harassment carries substantial penalties under the Marine Mammal Protection Act and Florida law.
- We observe manatee protection zones, idle-speed and no-wake zones, and seagrass avoidance. Expect the vessel to move slowly through them.

## 10. Dress

Swimwear, resort casual, or whatever the event calls for. Required: **flat, non-marking, non-slip footwear or bare feet.** Prohibited: heels, cleats, and anything that gouges an aluminum deck. Cover-ups on for boarding and disembarking at the dock.

## 11. Show up on time

The vessel departs at its scheduled time. Tides, bridge openings, dock slots, and Coast Guard timelines don't wait, and neither can we. Late arrivals are not accommodated and do not receive refunds. Check-in closes `[XX]` minutes before departure.

## 12. Dock and shoreside conduct

The marina is a working facility with neighbors, residents, and other operators. Park where directed. Keep noise down in the lot and on the dock. Don't board other vessels. Don't linger after disembarking. How we behave ashore determines whether we keep our dock privileges.

## 13. Damage

Guests are responsible for damage they cause to the vessel, its equipment, or the facility, whether deliberate or through negligence. Costs may be charged to the payment method on file.

## 14. Enforcement

Depending on severity, violations may result in a verbal warning, service refusal, restriction of movement aboard, return to dock, removal at the next safe opportunity, forfeiture of fees, suspension, permanent revocation of membership, and referral to law enforcement. Conduct listed in our Zero Tolerance Policies skips every intermediate step.

## 15. Speak up

If something happens aboard — to you or to someone else — tell any crew member immediately, or contact `[SAFETY OFFICER]` at `[CLUB EMAIL]` / `[CLUB PHONE]`. Reports are taken seriously and investigated. **Retaliation against anyone who makes a good-faith report is itself a zero-tolerance violation.**

# DOCUMENT 4 — ZERO TOLERANCE POLICIES

**M/V Sea Charm II · [un] Hinged Social**

Most of our rules come with room for a conversation. These do not.

The conduct below results in **immediate termination of participation, removal from the vessel at the first safe opportunity, permanent revocation of membership and all associated privileges, complete forfeiture of all fees paid, and referral to law enforcement where applicable.** There is no warning step, no progressive discipline, and no refund. These apply to members, guests, guests of guests, talent, vendors, contractors, and crew without distinction.

## 1. Drugs and controlled substances

Possession, use, distribution, or transport of any illegal drug or controlled substance — **including cannabis and THC products in every form, and including holders of Florida medical marijuana cards.** Federal law governs this vessel. The Coast Guard's zero-tolerance policy exposes the vessel itself to seizure over trace amounts. This rule protects our ability to operate at all.

## 2. Weapons

Possession of any firearm, ammunition, or weapon as defined in our Prohibited Items list. Concealed carry permits confer no authority aboard.

## 3. Sexual misconduct

Sexual assault, non-consensual physical contact, sexual harassment, indecent exposure, voyeurism, upskirting, or recording another person in a state of undress or in a private space. Reports are escalated to law enforcement.

## 4. Violence and threats

Fighting, striking, shoving, restraining, threatening, or intimidating any person aboard or on the dock. Aboard a vessel offshore, a physical altercation is a mass-casualty risk, not a scuffle.

## 5. Discrimination and hate conduct

Slurs, hate speech, harassment, or targeted conduct directed at any person on the basis of race, color, national origin, ethnicity, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, age, disability, or any other protected characteristic.

## 6. Interference with vessel operation

Touching, obstructing, or attempting to operate the helm, throttles, controls, machinery, or navigation equipment. Entering the helm station, engine spaces, or crew-only areas without authorization. Distracting or obstructing the Captain or crew during navigation, docking, or an emergency.

## 7. Tampering with safety equipment

Removing, hiding, damaging, deploying without cause, or misusing any life jacket, life ring, life float, fire extinguisher, EPIRB, flare, alarm, radio, or emergency light. **This is a federal offense.** Discharging a fire extinguisher as a joke ends your membership and starts a criminal referral.

## 8. False distress and radio misuse

Making a false distress call, hoax mayday, or misuse of the vessel's VHF radio or emergency beacon. This is a federal felony carrying prison time, substantial fines, and liability for the full cost of any resulting search and rescue response.

## 9. Unauthorized water entry

Jumping, diving, or entering the water while the vessel is underway, while engines are running, or outside an authorized swim call. Pushing or throwing another person into the water. Man-overboard incidents are the deadliest event on this class of vessel, and every one of them is preventable.

## 10. Underage drinking and furnishing alcohol to minors

Consumption of alcohol by anyone under 21, and — far more seriously — providing, purchasing, or facilitating alcohol for anyone under 21. **This is a felony under Florida Statute § 562.11.** Adults who supply minors are removed and reported without exception.

## 11. Identity fraud

Presenting false, altered, or borrowed identification. Misrepresenting your age. Boarding on another person's reservation or transferring a ticket in violation of our terms.

## 12. Concealed contraband

Attempting to conceal or smuggle aboard any prohibited item after screening — glass, weapons, controlled substances, or pyrotechnics. Bringing it is a refusal. Hiding it is a revocation.

## 13. Incapacitation

Arriving at or reaching a level of impairment where you cannot stand unassisted, respond to crew, comprehend instructions, or safely manage yourself aboard. This is a safety determination made by the Captain and crew, and it is final.

## 14. Refusal of a safety instruction

Declining, ignoring, or arguing with a direct safety instruction from the Captain or crew during an emergency, drill, swim call, docking, or severe weather event.

## 15. Overboard discharge

Discharging or throwing any waste, trash, cigarette, plastic, fuel, oil, or sewage into the water. Operating or tampering with any discharge valve.

## 16. Theft and vandalism

Theft from the vessel, crew, or other guests. Deliberate damage to the vessel, its equipment, or the dock facility.

## 17. Retaliation

Retaliating against, intimidating, doxxing, or attempting to identify any person who has made a good-faith report of a violation.

# DOCUMENT 5 — EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

**M/V Sea Charm II · Guest Emergency Reference**

Read this before you sail. In an emergency, the single most useful thing you can do is stay seated, stay quiet, and listen for crew instructions. Panic moves faster than water.

## Before departure — your responsibilities

1. **Attend the safety orientation.** Mandatory for every guest, every sailing.
2. **Know three things:** where your muster station is, where the life jackets are stowed, and where the two nearest exits are.
3. **Disclose medical conditions** at booking or check-in — cardiac conditions, epilepsy, diabetes, severe allergies, mobility limitations, pregnancy, non-swimmer status. This is confidential and it changes how we help you.
4. **Tell the crew if you cannot swim.** No one will comment on it. It matters enormously to us.
5. **Locate a crew member.** Crew wear `[IDENTIFIER]`.

## Emergency signals

| Signal | Meaning | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **7 or more short blasts + 1 prolonged blast** | General emergency / muster | Stop what you're doing. Move to your muster station. Await instructions. |
| **3 prolonged blasts** | Person overboard | Point continuously at the person. Do not look away. Shout "Man overboard!" |
| **Continuous sounding** | Fire or immediate danger | Move away from the affected area toward your muster station. |
| **Verbal PA announcement** | Any situation | Stop talking. Listen. Comply. |

## Person in the water

1. **Shout it out immediately** — "Man overboard, port side!" or "starboard side."
2. **Point at the person and keep pointing.** Do not stop. Do not look away. This is your entire job and it is genuinely the difference between recovery and loss.
3. **Throw a ring buoy or throwable device** toward them.
4. **Do not enter the water.** Untrained rescuers become second victims. The crew is trained and equipped.
5. Everyone else: **sit down and stay clear** so the crew can maneuver.

## Fire

1. Shout **"Fire, fire, fire!"** and state the location.
2. Alert the nearest crew member immediately.
3. Move away from the fire, upwind, toward your muster station.
4. **Do not attempt to fight the fire.** Crew are trained and the vessel carries approved extinguishing equipment.
5. Stay low if there is smoke.

## Flooding or grounding

1. Sit down and brace. Hold something fixed.
2. Do not rush to one side of the vessel — that is how a stable boat becomes an unstable one.
3. Await crew instruction. Don life jackets if directed.

## Abandon ship

Only on the **direct order of the Captain.** Never before.

1. Put on a life jacket and secure all straps.
2. Move to your muster station.
3. Stay with your group and follow crew direction to the exit point.
4. Enter feet first, one hand crossed over your chest holding the jacket collar, one hand covering your nose and mouth.
5. **Stay with the vessel or the group.** A pontoon vessel is far easier to spot from the air than a swimmer. Do not attempt to swim to shore — distance over water is deceiving.

## Medical emergency

1. **Notify crew immediately.** Do not wait to see if it improves.
2. The vessel carries a first aid kit, AED, and oxygen, and crew hold current CPR/AED certification.
3. The Captain coordinates response by **VHF Channel 16** and coordinates transport with the U.S. Coast Guard, Miami-Dade Fire Rescue, or Broward Sheriff's Office Fire Rescue marine units as appropriate.
4. Clear space. Give the crew room. Do not crowd.
5. If you know something relevant about the person — a condition, a medication, an allergy — say so immediately.

## Heat, sun, and dehydration

South Florida sun on an open aluminum deck is more intense than shore. Watch for dizziness, headache, nausea, cramping, confusion, or stopping sweating. Drink water continuously, use the shaded areas, and tell a crew member early. Heat illness escalates fast and is trivially manageable when caught early.

## Marine life injury

Jellyfish sting, sea lice, ray injury, or coral abrasion: exit the water and notify crew. Do not rinse with fresh water and do not rub the area. Crew carry appropriate treatment.

## Severe weather and lightning

Afternoon storms build rapidly here. The Captain monitors NWS Miami marine forecasts and radar continuously and will curtail or cancel any sailing on that basis.

If weather develops: move inside the covered deck, stay off rails and metal fittings, stay seated and low, and stop swimming immediately. Swim calls end at the first sign of lightning regardless of distance.

## Collision or sudden maneuver

Sit. Brace. Hold a fixed object. Stay seated until the crew says otherwise — a second impact or hard turn often follows the first.

## Loss of propulsion

The vessel carries twin engines and independent systems. Loss of one engine is a routine, manageable condition. Stay seated. The Captain will communicate.

## Accountability

Head counts are taken at boarding, before and after every swim call, and before departure from any stop. **When your name or number is called, answer audibly.** If you notice someone missing, tell a crew member immediately — do not assume someone else has.

## Emergency contacts

|---|---|
| On the water — all vessels and agencies | **VHF Channel 16** |
| Life-threatening emergency ashore | **911** |
| U.S. Coast Guard Sector Miami Command Center | `[VERIFY CURRENT NUMBER]` |
| [un] Hinged Social 24-hour emergency line | `[CLUB PHONE]` |

**Never make a distress call yourself.** The Captain controls all radio communication. False or duplicate distress calls divert rescue resources from real emergencies and carry federal criminal penalties.

## After any incident

Report every injury, however minor, to the crew before you disembark. This is for your benefit — undocumented injuries become disputed injuries. [un] Hinged Social files marine casualty reports with the U.S. Coast Guard as required under 46 CFR Part 4.
