# 2 · SERVICE ANIMAL POLICY

## 2.1 What qualifies

Under the ADA, a **service animal is a dog** (or in limited cases, a miniature horse) **individually trained to do work or perform tasks for a person with a disability.** Service animals are welcome aboard M/V Sea Charm II.

## 2.2 What does not qualify

**Emotional support animals, comfort animals, therapy animals, and companion animals are not service animals under the ADA and are not permitted aboard.** Neither are pets of any kind. This is not a judgment about the value of those animals — it is a capacity and safety limit on a small vessel where every animal aboard is one the crew must account for in an emergency.

Online registration certificates, ID cards, and vests confer no status. There is no legitimate service animal registry.

## 2.3 The two questions

Where a disability is not obvious, staff may ask only:

1. Is the animal required because of a disability?
2. What work or task has the animal been trained to perform?

Staff will not ask about your disability, request documentation, require a demonstration, or ask the animal to perform its task.

## 2.4 Handler responsibilities

- **Advance notice is required.** Notify `[CLUB EMAIL]` at least `[48]` hours before sailing so seating and capacity can be arranged.
- The animal remains harnessed, leashed, or tethered unless that interferes with its work, in which case it must be under voice or signal control at all times.
- The animal remains under your control and out of aisles, exits, and equipment access.
- You are responsible for the animal's care, feeding, and waste. Bring waste bags. Waste goes in a receptacle aboard — **never overboard.**
- The animal does not occupy a seat.
- You are responsible for damage the animal causes.

## 2.5 Removal

A service animal may be excluded only if it is out of control and the handler does not take effective action, or it is not housebroken. In that case the handler remains welcome to sail without the animal, or receives a full refund.

## 2.6 Safety realities to consider

We want you to make an informed decision: the deck gets hot, spray and swell are constant, there is no shaded ground surface, and an animal that goes overboard in open water is very hard to recover. Bring water and a mat. Consider whether the environment suits your animal.

## 2.7 Other guests

Allergy or fear of dogs is not grounds for excluding a service animal. Where possible, crew will seat affected parties apart.

## 4.2 What to include

Date and sailing, what happened, who was involved, who else saw it, what you'd like to see happen. Partial information is still worth submitting — send what you have.

## 4.3 Timeline

| Stage | Target |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgment | `[2]` business days |

## 4.4 How investigations run

- Handled by `[SAFETY OFFICER]` or, where they are implicated, by `[ALTERNATE]`

- Witnesses and any available records are reviewed
- Standard of proof: **more likely than not**

Pending investigation, the Company may separate parties on future sailings, suspend booking privileges, or restrict access. Interim measures are protective, not punitive, and imply no finding.

## 4.7 Confidentiality

Handled discreetly, though absolute confidentiality cannot be promised — some reports require action that reveals their existence. Where a reporter asks that no action be taken, the Company will honor that where it safely can, and will explain plainly when it cannot.

## 4.8 Retaliation

Retaliating against anyone who reports in good faith, or who participates in an investigation, is a Zero Tolerance violation. This applies to members, guests, crew, staff, and leadership without distinction.

## 4.9 Bad faith reports

Knowingly false reports made to harm another person are themselves a violation. Reports that turn out to be mistaken are not.

# 5 · PROGRESSIVE DISCIPLINE & APPEALS

**The space between "please stop" and "never come back."** Zero Tolerance violations bypass this entirely and go straight to permanent revocation.

## 5.1 Levels

| Level | Trigger | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Verbal correction** | Minor, first-time, immediately corrected | Crew instruction on the spot. Logged only if repeated. |
| **2 · Documented warning** | Repeat minor, or a first moderate violation | Written notice. Logged. Counts toward escalation for `[12 months]`. |
| **3 · Service restriction** | Conduct aboard requiring intervention | Alcohol service ended, movement restricted, or seating reassigned for the remainder of the sailing. Logged. |
| **4 · Removal from sailing** | Conduct the Master judges unsafe or unacceptable | Return to dock or disembarkation at the next safe point. Full fare forfeited. Logged. |
| **5 · Suspension** | Third documented warning in `[12 months]`, or a serious single incident | Booking privileges suspended `[30 / 90 / 180]` days. Written notice with reasoning. |
| **6 · Permanent revocation** | Repeat serious conduct, or any Zero Tolerance violation | Membership terminated. Permanent ban from all events and property. No refund of dues or fees. |

## 5.2 What escalates a response

Prior record · risk created to others · whether crew were obstructed · whether alcohol or substances were involved · whether the guest complied when corrected · whether the conduct targeted a protected characteristic · whether the guest was untruthful about it afterward.

## 5.3 What de-escalates

Immediate compliance · voluntary disclosure before being caught · genuine accountability · a clean record · restitution.

## 5.4 The rolling window

Documented warnings age out after `[12 months]` of clean standing. Suspensions and revocations do not age out.

## 5.5 Sponsor consequences

Where a guest brought by a member is disciplined at Level 4 or above, the sponsoring member receives a documented warning and may face guest privilege restriction. See the Member Sponsorship Policy.

## 5.6 Appeals

**Eligible:** Levels 4, 5, and 6.
**Not eligible:** Levels 1–3, and any revocation arising from violence, sexual misconduct, weapons, controlled substances, or criminal conduct.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| Filing | Written, to `[CLUB EMAIL]`, within **14 days** of notice |
| Contents | What you believe was wrong, and any information not previously considered |
| Reviewer | `[REVIEWING BODY]` — must not include anyone involved in the original decision |
| Timeline | Written decision within `[21]` days |
| Outcome | Upheld · reduced · overturned · returned for further investigation |
| Finality | The appeal decision is final |

Filing an appeal does not suspend the sanction. Suspensions and bans remain in force during review.

## 5.7 Records

Disciplinary records are retained `[5 years]`, or permanently for revocations, and are treated as confidential under the Privacy Policy.

# 6 · LANGUAGE ACCESS POLICY

## 6.1 Why this exists

We operate in a county where a majority of residents speak a language other than English at home. **A safety briefing a guest cannot understand is not a safety briefing** — it is a compliance formality that will fail at the exact moment it matters. This policy is a safety measure first and a hospitality measure second.

## 6.2 Priority languages

**English · Spanish · Haitian Creole**, with `[Portuguese]` as a secondary priority given the market.

## 6.3 Translation tiers

| Tier | Documents | Standard |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Safety critical** | Emergency Procedures · Safety orientation script · Life jacket and emergency placards · Muster instructions · Prohibited Items | Professional human translation. **No machine translation.** Reviewed by a native speaker with marine vocabulary. |
| **Tier 2 — Conduct and terms** | Code of Conduct · Zero Tolerance · Allowed Items · Weather & Cancellation | Professional human translation |
| **Tier 3 — Supporting** | What to Expect · Sandbar & Swim Call · Environmental Pledge · booking flow | Professional translation as resources allow |
| **Tier 4 — Governance** | Terms of Carriage · Waiver · Bylaws | Translated for comprehension, with English designated as the controlling version. `ATTORNEY — confirm this designation is properly disclosed.` |

## 6.4 Aboard the vessel

- Safety placards are **pictographic wherever possible** — a life jacket diagram works in every language
- Crew language capabilities are noted on the crew roster; where a Spanish- or Creole-speaking crew member is aboard, they are identified to guests at orientation
- The safety orientation is delivered in `[English and Spanish]` where a Spanish-speaking crew member is on watch
- Written safety cards in all Tier 1 languages are available at every seat or on request

## 6.5 At booking

Guests may indicate a language preference. Confirmations, weather notices, and cancellation alerts are sent in that language where available.

## 6.6 Interpretation

Where a guest requires interpretation not available from crew, the Company will `[use a telephone interpretation service / permit a companion to interpret]`. A companion interpreting for a guest does not satisfy the safety orientation requirement on their own — crew confirm comprehension directly.

## 6.7 Hiring

Multilingual capability is a stated preference in crew and staff recruitment. In this market it is an operational asset, not a nice-to-have.
